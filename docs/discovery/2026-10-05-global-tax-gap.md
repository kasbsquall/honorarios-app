# Global tax set-aside gap for cross-border freelancers

Question: is "paid in full by a foreign client, no tax withheld, then unable to cover the prepayment because the money was spent" a problem beyond Peru?

All sources accessed 2026-10-05. Where a page could not be opened directly and the fact comes from a search-engine snippet of an official page, this is flagged as "(snippet)".

## 1. Market size

The World Bank report "Working Without Borders: The Promise and Peril of Online Gig Work" (2023) estimates 154 to 435 million online gig workers, 4.4% to 12.5% of the global workforce. The upper figure includes people doing gig work as secondary or marginal activity; the survey-based estimate of people for whom it is the main job is 132.5 million.
Source: https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/ebc4a7e2-85c6-467b-8713-e2d77e954c6c/full (snippet) and report PDF https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/e538b081e65bd5f60f6f281dbcca7967-0460012023/original/Working-Without-Borders-The-Promise-and-Peril-of-Online-Gig-Work.pdf (snippet).

The World Bank blog of 7 September 2023 confirms "up to 435 million people who engage in online gig work" and that the online gig economy accounts for "up to 12 percent of the global labor market". It does not discuss taxation.
Source: https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/voices/promise-and-peril-online-gig-work-developing-countries (fetched).

Caveat: these figures cover all online gig work, domestic and cross-border. We found no official figure isolating freelancers paid by foreign clients.

Payoneer's 2023 Freelancer Insights Report surveyed over 2,000 freelancers in 122 countries; 32% said they had expanded their client base to new countries. It is a company survey, not official statistics, and has no data on taxes.
Source: https://www.payoneer.com/resources/2023-global-freelancer-income-report/ (snippet).

## 2. Countries where the freelancer must self-prepay

|---|---|---|---|
| United States | Estimated tax (Form 1040-ES), quarterly | Required if you expect to owe "tax of $1,000 or more when their return is filed". | United Kingdom | Self Assessment payments on account | "Each payment is usually half of the tax you owed the previous year", due 31 January and 31 July. | India | Advance tax | Due if liability exceeds Rs 10,000 in the year; instalments of at least 15% (15 June), 45% (15 Sept), 75% (15 Dec), 100% (15 March). | https://incometaxindia.gov.in/Documents/Tax-Calendar/Payment-of-Advance-Tax.htm and https://incometaxindia.gov.in/tutorials/31.%20provisions%20on%20pymt%20of%20adv.%20tax.pdf (snippet; site returned 403 to direct fetch) |

Colombia (anticipo de renta: 25% first year, 50% second, 75% thereafter, Estatuto Tributario art. 807) was only found in press coverage (Portafolio, Valora Analitik). The official Senado page refused connection. Treat as unverified until checked at secretariasenado.gov.co or dian.gov.co.

Argentina, Nigeria and Kenya were not researched in this pass.

## 3. Evidence that people fail to set money aside or get penalised

IRS Tax Gap Projections for Tax Year 2022 (Publication 5869): gross tax gap $696 billion. Of this, underpayment (tax reported on time but not paid on time) is $94 billion. Business income is the largest contributor within individual underreporting, at 28% of the gross gap.
Source: https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p5869.pdf and https://www.irs.gov/statistics/irs-the-tax-gap (snippet; PDF could not be parsed directly). Caveat: the $94 billion covers all taxpayers, not only freelancers.

HMRC, 9 December 2025: 17,955 Self Assessment customers set up an online Time to Pay arrangement between 6 April and 30 November 2025.
Source: https://www.gov.uk/government/news/hmrc-offers-time-to-help-pay-your-tax-bill (fetched). This shows demand for paying the bill in instalments; it does not state the cause.

The IRS itself warns that the estimated tax penalty can apply even when a refund is due at year end, meaning the timing of payment, separate from the total amount, is penalised.
Source: https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estimated-taxes (fetched).

Secondary only: articles citing a QuickBooks Self-Employed survey say 42% of freelancers who did not pay came up short at the deadline and 30% did not make enough to pay in full; a SCORE blog headline says 36% of entrepreneurs are not filing. We did not reach the original survey and these numbers should not be used as-is.
Source: https://www.score.org/blog/why-36-percent-entrepreneurs-arent-filing-taxes (snippet, secondary).

All products found are tied to US banking and US tax rules. None was found serving a freelancer resident in Peru, Brazil, India or the Philippines who is paid by a foreign client.

## What we could not verify

- A figure for how many freelancers are paid specifically by foreign clients (the World Bank number covers all online gig work).
- ILO figures; not reached in this pass.
- Number of US taxpayers assessed the estimated tax penalty (IRC 6654) per year; the Taxpayer Advocate PDF did not yield readable numbers.
- The original QuickBooks Self-Employed survey and its year and sample.
- Colombia art. 807 from an official source; Argentina, Nigeria and Kenya not researched.
- Any official statistic, in any country, on prepayment default specific to people paid from abroad.
- Whether Found, Lili or Collective accept non-US residents without a US entity.

## What this means for positioning

1. The obligation to self-prepay is standard across at least seven tax systems (US, UK, India, Brazil, Mexico, Philippines, Peru), so the Peru pain is one instance of a general pattern.
2. Official data shows late payment is material (IRS: $94 billion of tax reported but not paid on time; HMRC: about 18,000 online payment plans in eight months), though none of it isolates cross-border freelancers.
3. Automatic set-aside already exists and is proven as a feature, but every product found is US-banked and US-tax-specific.
4. The open space is a set-aside that happens at the moment a cross-border payment lands, using local rules, for freelancers outside the US banking system.
5. Claims about failure rates should stay qualitative until a primary survey is found; the strongest numbers available are official tax gap and payment-plan figures.
